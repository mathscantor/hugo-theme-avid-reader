Austen’s comedy of manners is sharper than its reputation as a romance. Every visit, letter, and ball is a small social calculation, and the pleasure is watching Elizabeth Bennet refuse to be calculated.

The first half is almost architectural: families arranged, incomes announced, reputations stacked like china. Then the book turns, and the same details that felt decorative become evidence. Darcy’s letter is the hinge. After it, rereading is a different sport.

{{< pullquote attribution="Elizabeth Bennet" >}}
I declare after all there is no enjoyment like reading!
{{< /pullquote >}}

What I keep is the tone. Austen is funny without winking, moral without preaching, and still one of the best arguments for paying attention to people.
