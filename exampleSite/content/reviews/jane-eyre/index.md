Jane’s voice is the event. She is poor, plain, and unwilling to be grateful for crumbs, which makes the novel feel more radical than its marriage plot. Lowood is still hard to read. Thornfield is a fairy tale with a locked room.

Rochester is theatre. Jane is judgment. The book keeps asking whether love that requires you to disappear is love at all. I like that it answers without becoming tidy. The moor chapters, after the flight, are the moral center: independence first, then return.

{{< pullquote attribution="Jane Eyre" >}}
I am no bird; and no net ensnares me.
{{< /pullquote >}}

A gothic romance that is also a manifesto for a self.
