Wells invades the commuter belt. The terror is not the Martians’ intelligence so much as the sudden smallness of Surrey. Heat-rays arrive before institutions can invent a tone of voice for them. That still feels modern.

The narrator is ordinary on purpose. He runs, hides, and reports. The artilleryman’s speech about a new underground humanity is the book’s coldest idea: catastrophe as an excuse to stop being decent.

{{< pullquote attribution="The narrator" >}}
Yet so vain is man, and so blinded by his vanity, that no writer, up to the very end of the nineteenth century, expressed any idea that intelligent life might have developed there far, or indeed at all, beyond its earthly level.
{{< /pullquote >}}

An invasion novel that is really a study of panic, and of how quickly a landscape can become someone else’s.
