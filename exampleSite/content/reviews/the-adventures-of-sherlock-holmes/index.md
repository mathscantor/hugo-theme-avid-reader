Doyle’s gift is pace. Each story sets a small, slightly crooked world on the table, lets Holmes tilt it toward the light, and is done before the trick goes stale. Watson is the secret engine: without his admiration, Holmes would be only a machine.

The pleasures are domestic as much as deductive. Fog, hansom cabs, a client who will not sit still. “A Scandal in Bohemia” remains the best of them because Holmes is allowed to lose something he cannot classify.

{{< pullquote attribution="Sherlock Holmes" >}}
You see, but you do not observe.
{{< /pullquote >}}

I reread these when I want competence without gloom. The crimes are puzzles; the friendship is the plot.
