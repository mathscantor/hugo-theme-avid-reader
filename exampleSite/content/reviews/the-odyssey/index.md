A homecoming story that keeps postponing home. Odysseus is clever, cruel, tired, and very good at surviving other people’s hospitality. The poem is just as interested in the household that waits: Telemachus learning a voice, Penelope holding a room together with a loom.

I read it as a book about storytelling itself. Odysseus narrates half his life to strangers in order to eat and sleep. The monsters are memorable; the recognition scenes are better. A scar, a bed built from a tree, a dog that dies of relief.

{{< pullquote attribution="Odysseus" >}}
There is nothing more admirable than when two people who see eye to eye keep house as man and wife, confounding their enemies and delighting their friends.
{{< /pullquote >}}

Old, strange, and still the best map of how long it can take to get back to a familiar door.
