Wilde writes aphorisms the way other people breathe, then lets a plot punish the man who believed them. Lord Henry is the book’s brightest voice and its cheapest philosophy. Dorian is what happens when style is asked to do the work of a conscience.

The portrait is a simple supernatural device, almost a stage trick. The novel around it is about influence: who speaks, who listens, who gets to remain untouched. Basil’s affection is the only sincere thing in the room, which is why the room cannot stand it.

{{< pullquote attribution="Lord Henry" >}}
The only way to get rid of a temptation is to yield to it.
{{< /pullquote >}}

Cruel, glittering, and shorter than its shadow. I like it best when it stops being witty and becomes afraid.
