This is not a novel that happens to include whale facts. It is a mind that cannot stop naming the world, and a hunt that turns that naming into fate. Ishmael begins companionably, almost like a travel writer. Ahab arrives and the sentences lengthen into sermons.

The cetology chapters are not obstacles. They are the book teaching you how it wants to be read: slowly, sideways, with room for obsession. Skip them and you get a simpler revenge story. Stay, and the whale becomes a problem of knowledge.

{{< pullquote attribution="Ishmael" >}}
I know not all that may be coming, but be it what it will, I'll go to it laughing.
{{< /pullquote >}}

I finished it exhausted and oddly cheerful. Few books make the sea feel both workplace and metaphysics.
