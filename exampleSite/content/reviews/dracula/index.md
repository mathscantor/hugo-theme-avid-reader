Stoker builds dread out of paperwork. Diaries, telegrams, phonograph cylinders: the vampire is hunted by a committee with good filing habits. That should be funny, and sometimes it is, but the early castle chapters still work. Jonathan Harker’s politeness is the first trap.

Mina is the book’s intelligence. Van Helsing supplies folklore; she supplies method. The later pursuit across Europe is baggier than the opening, yet the scrapbook form keeps making the threat feel local and modern.

{{< pullquote attribution="Jonathan Harker" >}}
I am all in a sea of wonders. I doubt; I fear; I think strange things, which I dare not confess to my own soul.
{{< /pullquote >}}

A novel about information as much as blood, and still unnerving when the Count is only a hand on a window.
